The Effects of Floods and Landslides in Myanmar in July 2015

In July 2015, Myanmar experienced severe floods and landslides caused by continuous heavy monsoon rainfall. The situation becamme worse Cyclone Komen brought additional heavy rain and strong winds to the country at the end of July. These disasters caused widespread damage across 12 of Myanmar’s 14 states and regions, including Kachin, Chin Rakhine, Sagaing, Magway, Ayeyarwady, Bago, and Yangon. According to disaster reports, approximately 1.6 million people were temporarily displaced, and 132 people lost their lives. Many families had to leave their homes and move to temporary shelters because of rising floodwaters and landslides. The floods and landslides damaged nearly 525,000 houses, including approximately 39,00 houses that were destroyed. Roads and bridges were also damaged, making transportation and rescue operations difficult. Furthermore, more than 1.14 million acres of farmland were affected, causing farmers to lose crops, income, and food supplies. To help the affefcted communities, the Myanmar Government, local volunteers, and international humantarian organizations provided emergency assistance, including food, clean water, medical supplies, and temporary shelters. Recovery efforts also focused on helping farmer and families rebuild their livelihoods. In conclusion, the 2015 floods and landslides were a major natural disaster in Myanmar. They caused loss of life, damages infrastructure, and affected agriculture and the economy. This disaster highlighted the importance of early-warning systems and effective disaster preparedness to reducce the impact of future floods and landslides.
